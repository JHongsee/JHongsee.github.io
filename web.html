Optimizing Zone Management for ZNS-Based Key-Value Stores via SSTfile Lifetime Awareness

JaeHong Bae

Pusan National University bjh0820@pusan.ac.kr

1. Background & Research Motivation

As the demand for large-scale data processing increases, key-value stores have been widely used as storage engines thanks to their high performance and flexibility. To minimize GC overhead, next-generation ZNS SSDs delegate zone management directly to the host. ZenFS is a user-level file system designed to run RocksDB in such ZNS environments, operating under two primary management policies:

Zone Allocation Policy

The lifetime hint of a zone is determined by the first SSTfile written to it. ZenFS compares the incoming SSTfile's lifetime with existing zones, selecting the one that has a longer but closest lifetime. If space is insufficient, a new zone is allocated.

  • Lifetime Matching: Groups SSTfiles with similar lifespans into the same zone.
  • Simultaneous Invalidation: Guides all data within a zone to expire simultaneously, enabling a full hardware reset without any data copy overhead.
  • GC Overhead Minimization: Minimizes the remaining valid data to drastically reduce block copy overhead during garbage collection, even if a direct zone reset is not achieved due to incomplete simultaneous invalidation.

Zone Reset Policy

ZenFS periodically executes a zone reset algorithm to reclaim storage space. This process is primarily triggered when SSTfiles are deleted due to background compactions, rendering the data inside the zone invalid.

  • Full Device Scanning: Scans all active zones within the ZNS SSD at specific intervals.
  • Zero-Valid Reset: Resets any zone that contains absolutely no valid remaining data.
  • Proactive Reclamation: Secures empty zones early to delay garbage collection.

⚠️ Limitations of ZenFS & Our Motivation

Despite its architectural design, the default zone management in ZenFS introduces critical overheads due to its lifetime-agnostic behavior, suffering from the following limitations:

  • Over-allocation: Excessive zone reservation for long-lifetime SST files under the default allocation logic.
  • Data Mixing: Frequent zone reset delays and garbage collection triggering caused by mixing multi-lifetime SST files within identical zones.
  • Endurance Degradation: Reduced ZNS SSD hardware lifespan due to indiscriminate zone resets executed without tracking spatial zone utilization.

2. Research Content & Methodology

To address the aforementioned limitations of ZenFS, we propose an SSTfile lifetime-aware zone management scheme. Our system architecture model core operations are divided into two main components: Strong Lifetime based Allocation (SLA) and Lazy Zone Reset (LZR).

Method 1

Strong Lifetime-based Allocation (SLA)

SLA is a policy that co-locates SSTfiles with identical lifetimes into the same zone. By synchronizing the invalidation timing of data based on their specific lifespans, this approach aims to maximize zone reset efficiency while suppressing the unnecessary consumption of empty zones. Isolating SSTfiles with matching lifetimes ensures simultaneous expiration, allowing these zones to be reset immediately and effectively reclaiming available storage resources.

  • Strict Lifetime Co-location: Places SSTfiles with identical expected lifespans into the same dedicated zones.
  • Synchronized Invalidation: Aligns the exact expiration timing of data to enable immediate, full hardware zone resets.
  • Resource Optimization: Prevents the premature exhaustion of empty zones and significantly reduces system overhead.
Method 2

Lazy Zone Reset (LZR) for Short-Lifetime Zones

Under the SLA policy, analysis reveals that while zones with a lifetime hint of 3 or higher achieve nearly 100% space utilization upon hardware reset, lifetime-2 zones are reset with only about 12% utilization. This inefficiency stems from RocksDB's logging mechanism: Write-Ahead Logs (WAL) have extremely short lifespans, as they are immediately deleted once memory-resident MemTables flush data to the ZNS SSD. Placed into lifetime-2 zones under SLA, these WAL files trigger zero-valid reset conditions before the zone can be fully filled. This premature, repetitive resetting unnecessarily inflates the total reset count of the ZNS SSD. To maximize space efficiency, we propose LZR (Lazy Zone Reset), which proactively defers the hardware reset of unfilled short-lifetime zones even when they contain no valid data.

  • Underutilization Discovery: Identifies that short-lived zones (lifetime 2) suffer from premature resets at only ~12% capacity due to transient RocksDB WAL operations.
  • Deferred Reset Mechanism: Postpones the hardware-level zone reset for partially filled, short-lifetime zones to suppress redundant reset commands.
  • Space Maximization: Maximizes storage space utilization and effectively improves overall ZNS SSD endurance by reducing unnecessary device-level reset cycles.

3. Results & Performance

We evaluate the performance of our proposed lifetime-aware zone management scheme (SLA + LZR) against the Default ZenFS baseline. The evaluation focuses on write throughput efficiency, zone reset frequency, and transactional execution times across distinct workloads.

fillrandom Analysis

The fillrandom workload is characterized by injecting key-value inputs via random write operations.

  • Workload Characterization: The fillrandom workload evaluates system behavior under intensive, randomized key-value write streams.
  • Efficiency Enhancement: By mitigating over-allocation and exploiting SSTfile lifetime behaviors, SLA+LZR maintains a superior free-space ratio compared to Default ZenFS.
  • Overhead & Performance Optimization: Efficient space reclamation drastically reduces garbage collection overhead, thereby boosting overall throughput and maximizing storage zone utilization.

mixgraph Analysis

The mixgraph workload is a more realistic workload constructed by analyzing actual storage traces. It features highly frequent updates due to a large volume of requests accessing hot keys, and injects a diverse distribution of value sizes.

  • Cumulative & Interval Throughput: Evaluates both the overall average throughput and the critical interval throughput captured 1 second prior to workload completion.
  • Throughput Stability Gap: While Default ZenFS suffers from severe throughput drops in the later stages due to aggressive garbage collection, SLA+LZR sustains a highly stable and consistent throughput timeline until the very end of the workload.
  • Reset Optimization & Zero-GC: SLA+LZR triggers zero garbage collection overhead, managing to efficiently reclaim and secure ample device-level free space solely through our optimized zone reset policy.

YCSB Analysis

The YCSB Benchmark evaluates the macro-level efficiency of storage engines under standard cloud backend workloads. It demonstrates and verifies system performance by directly comparing total execution times within realistic transactional environments.

  • Execution Time Reduction: SLA+LZR successfully shortens the overall execution time across most evaluation workloads compared to Default ZenFS.
  • Mitigated Over-allocation: Prevents excessive zone allocation, allowing the storage framework to manage active physical blocks with higher structural efficiency.
  • GC Overhead Elimination: Consistent with previous experimental insights, maximizing free space reclamation effectively reduces garbage collection overhead to drive substantial macro-level performance leaps.
